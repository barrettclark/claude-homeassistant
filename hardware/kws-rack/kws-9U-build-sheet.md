## Assembly

### 1. Print the parts and lay them out

Legs, connectors, handles, the heavy-duty shelf and the magnet inserts. PETG, strength-first settings.

- Print the frame parts in PETG. PLA softens next to running equipment and will creep under load over time.
- Use a strength profile: more perimeters rather than more infill. The legs and the bottom connectors carry the weight.
- The magnet inserts print separately and snap into the legs later, so the magnets stay adjustable.

### 2. Heat-set the M5 inserts into every leg

Done first, before anything is joined. Lay the leg flat, inner side up, and press three inserts per U straight down into the mounting wall.

- The pockets open on the inner side of the leg. Iron at about 230 °C; let the insert sink until it is flush. It must not stand proud on either side.
- Pressed from the inside, a device screw pulls the insert against the wall instead of out of it.
- Use the 7 or 7.5 mm OD inserts the holes are sized for.
- Hardware: M5 brass inserts, 8 mm long: 12 per U
- Tip: Printing the nut-based frame variant instead? Skip this step entirely.

### 3. Snap the magnets in

Two 10 × 5 mm magnets per snap-in insert, seven inserts per 6U leg (one per 3U leg). The loaded insert then slides into its slot in the leg from the side.

- Drop two magnets into each insert, poles as marked: one column all +, the other all −, so they never repel each other.
- Slide the loaded insert sideways into its slot on the leg until it snaps. It snaps out again, so magnets can be added or moved later.
- Hardware: Magnets 10 × 5 mm (optional)

### 4. Build the bottom frame

Front connector, front legs, nuts, screws; the same at the back; then the side connectors close the ring. A nut goes in right before its screw: nothing holds it until then.

- Front connector flat, the two front legs in its pockets. Two nuts into each leg foot from the inside, nylon ring toward you; four M6 × 8 from the front.
- Same at the back.
- Three nuts into each end of the side connectors, slide them in from the sides, six M6 × 10 per side from the outside of the legs.
- Check the diagonals match before tightening everything.
- Hardware: M6 nyloc nuts: 8 in the legs, 12 in the side connectors; M6 × 8 mm: 8 for the front and back connectors; M6 × 10 mm: 12 for the side connectors

### 5. Drop in the heavy-duty shelf

The shelf rests on the bottom connectors and takes the heaviest device in the rack.

- Slide the shelf in from the top and let it settle on the lips of the bottom connectors.
- Fit the two stoppers so the shelf cannot creep forward when you push a device in.
- P1S or other smaller build plates: use the split heavy-duty base file; three mounting ears are enough, the base leans on the bottom legs.

### 6. Stack the next section

The next four legs sit on keyed joints. Each corner is then tied with a face plate and a side plate: nuts first, then the plate, then its screws.

- Set the next four legs on top of the base legs. The joints are keyed, so they only fit one way.
- At each corner, press two nuts into the top of the lower leg and two into the foot of the upper leg, from the inside. Bolt the face plate across the joint with four M6 × 8 from the front (or back).
- Press three nuts into each half of a side plate, hold it over the outer side of the joint and drive six M6 × 10 from the outside of the legs. A mid brace takes the place of the two side plates on its side, with twelve nuts and screws.
- Repeat for every 6U or 3U section.
- Hardware: Per joint: 16 nuts in the legs + 24 in the side plates; M6 × 8 mm: 16 for the face plates; M6 × 10 mm: 24 for the side plates

### 7. Slide the side plates down the rails

Before the top goes on: each side plate drops into the 3 mm rail that runs along the outer face of the legs, from the top down. The handles will close the rails and lock the plates.

- Cut the plates to the measured pitch, not the nominal 44.45 mm per U. The Side plates step has the numbers and the DXF.
- Line a plate up with the rails of the front and back legs and slide it down to the bottom connector. It should move freely; a plate that has to be forced is too wide.
- Nothing holds the plates yet. The handles in the next step close the top of the rails.
- Printed side panels are included with the model files if you would rather not laser-cut.

### 8. Assemble the top and close the rack

The 3 mm top plate slides into the grooves of the two handle halves; the halves close onto it from both sides. That whole top then comes down over the legs and locks the side plates in their rails. The top connectors tie it all to the legs.

- Press six nuts into each handle half, three at each leg, and add a drop of epoxy to each. With the side plates in, you can never reach these nuts again, so glue them now.
- Hold the top plate (229 × 201 mm, acrylic from the DXF or printed) with the fan opening toward the back, and slide the right and left handle halves onto its edges: the plate sits in the groove that runs along the inside of each half.
- Lower the assembled top, plate and both halves, onto the legs. The halves meet in the middle and close the top of the side-plate rails. Drive six M6 × 10 per half from the outside of the legs, through the leg wall into the handle nuts.
- Press two nuts into the top of each leg, front and back faces, from the inside. Bolt the front and back top connectors through the legs with four M6 × 8 each.
- Hardware: M6 nyloc nuts: 12 in the handles (glued), 8 in the legs; M6 × 10 mm: 12 for the handles; M6 × 8 mm: 8 for the top connectors; A drop of epoxy on each handle nut
- Tip: Glue the nuts inside the handles: future maintenance with the side plates installed becomes much easier.

### 9. Fit the fan and the grill

The Ø136 mm opening in the top plate and its four Ø4.5 mm holes on a 124.5 mm square take a 140 mm fan and the printed grill.

- Bolt the grill, with a 140 mm fan underneath if the rack runs warm, through the four holes with the fan screws. The plate itself is held by the handles; nothing else bolts it.
- No fan? Use the solid plate: the “Top plate no Fan” profile, or the solid DXF from the plates step, and skip this step.
- Hardware: 4 fan screws for a 140 mm fan or the grill

### 10. Mount your devices

Standard 10-inch gear bolts to the front legs with M5 × 10 screws into the inserts, four to six per panel.

- Front mount spacing is 260 mm, rear 244 mm. Inner rail spacing is 258 mm, outer 288 mm.
- Heaviest device on the shelf at the bottom; it keeps the rack planted.
- My modules (screen, Pi cluster, PSU shelf, patch panel, Synology module) all share this mounting pattern.
- Hardware: M5 × 10 mm: about 8 per U
